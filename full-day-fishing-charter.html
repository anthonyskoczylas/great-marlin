The day

Lines off at seven. Fishing before nine.

You meet us on the beach in Herradura at six forty-five, a small panga runs you out to the boat, and by seven the lines are off. A normal full day runs six to eight hours from there.

The run is twenty-two to twenty-eight miles, forty-five minutes to an hour. Then the bottom is simply gone underneath you, about a mile down, and the hunt starts: dredges and teasers to raise a sailfish, the tower reading the water for birds and bait, and a circle-hooked ballyhoo dropped in at the last second. That is bait-and-switch, the house method on this coast.

On the run home we often pitch the rocks and the beach for roosterfish. Meat fish are cleaned and bagged on the boat. Billfish are released in the water, every time.

A good peak-season day is four to twelve sailfish bites.

Green season, two to six. Sailfish throw hooks and win fights, so you will typically release about half of them. Those are honest charter numbers, not tournament numbers.

What you can catch

Seasons for this stretch of water

  • Pacific sailfishDec–Apr peak · around all yearThe reason this coast is famous, and the fish we plan the day around.
  • Yellowfin tunaMay–Sep peak · most of the yearFound under the spinner dolphin. Sashimi on the ride home.
  • Mahi mahiSep–Nov peak · green seasonColour, acrobatics, dinner.
  • MarlinJun–Sep · blue and black · a bonusThe real fishery is sixty miles further out. On a day trip, a blue is luck.
  • WahooMay–Jun peakThe fastest strike in the spread.

What’s included

Aboard

  • The boat and crew, your group only, nobody else aboard.
  • Lunch, beer, water and fruit, aboard for the day.
  • The fishing licence, paperwork goes through the boat; about $15 per angler, set by INCOPESCA.
  • Berths, a galley sink and a private head, shade below deck when someone needs it.
  • Your meat fish, cleaned and bagged on the boat.

Bring

  • Cash in dollars, for the crew tip, and the balance if you settle on the day.
  • Seasickness medication, the night before and again that morning.
  • Sun cover, hat, sunglasses, sunscreen.

Not included

  • The crew tip, 15–20% of the charter is customary, in cash, if the crew earned it.
  • Transfers to the beach, we can set them up on the same booking thread.

Questions

  • Is the full day the sailfish trip?

    Yes. The fish are forty-five minutes to an hour away, so the full day is the offshore trip. If sailfish are what you came for, this is the day to book.

  • How many people can come?

    The price covers up to six guests. Each guest beyond six is $100, up to eight aboard.

  • Can we keep what we catch?

    Yes, everything except billfish and roosterfish. Tuna, dorado, wahoo, snapper and grouper come back cleaned and bagged on the boat. Sailfish, marlin and roosterfish are always released.

  • Can we bring kids?

    Yes, and the cabin, berths and private head help. Six to eight hours offshore is a long day for young children, though, and the half day is often the better family trip.
